01

The one idea

Most dark-purple interfaces are a neutral grey UI with a violet button glued on. Ultraviolet inverts that: the background, the borders, the text and the accent are all the same hue. Only the chroma changes — from 0.008 in body copy to 0.21 in the accent. Nothing is grey. Everything is purple, most of it just very quietly.

rule 01

Chroma is energy

Quiet things don't change colour when you touch them — they get more purple. The accent is already at maximum chroma, so it's the one thing that brightens instead.

rule 02

Any other hue is news

Green, amber and red are the only non-300 hues in the system, and they only ever mean status. That's why they read so loudly.

rule 03

Dark ink on the accent

White on bright purple is ~3:1 — it fails AA and looks muddy. Near-black gives 7:1. The accessible choice is also the flatter, more poster-like one.

Two voices

Monospace is the machine talking: subdomains, IDs, statuses, counts, timestamps, paths, keys. Sans is the interface talking to you: descriptions, empty states, errors, help. If you can't decide which a string is, ask whether a human wrote it or a process emitted it.

The mark — two cuts of one drawing

The badge is derived, not redrawn. It's sad-pepe.svg with the nine stroke-only detail paths dropped, strokes removed, and the viewBox cropped to the face. Those hairlines were what turned the mark to mud; losing them and doubling the effective scale of the features is what buys legibility. Same geometry, same character.

05

Motion

One entry motion, used everywhere: fade up 6px. Volume comes from staggering it, not from inventing new animations. And a split that mirrors the type rule — you get springs, the machine gets eases. Anything you pressed responds like a physical object; anything the system did glides in.
